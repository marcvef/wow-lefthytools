# LefthyTools Money Data

What LefthyTools' WhyPoor? needs to know about making money in the world: the creatures worth farming
(how they react to each side, where they spawn), gathering nodes on every zone map (the skill they need,
what they give, where), fishing in every zone, what picking pockets gives, what disenchanting gives and
which vendors sell items in limited stock. It's an add-on of its own (load on demand: LefthyTools loads
it the first time WhyPoor? needs it) because it's under a different license from LefthyTools.

What a mob drops, its coin, levels and health aren't here: they're in
[LefthyTools_DropData](../LefthyTools_DropData/) (the Bestiary's), which WhyPoor? reads too.

## Where it comes from

`MoneyData.lua` is generated by `tools/update-money-data.js` from:

- [CMaNGOS Classic DB](https://github.com/cmangos/classic-db), the Full_DB dump
  `ClassicDB_1_12_1_z2815.sql.gz` (GPL v3): creatures and objects, their spawns and spawn pools, the
  loot tables (gathering, fishing, pickpocketing, disenchanting), the fishing skill per area, vendors'
  limited stock. A classic (1.12) world database: WoW: Forever is classic content with the same IDs, but
  its own additions aren't in there and its numbers may differ. Everything WhyPoor? shows from here is
  labelled as the classic database's.
- WoW: Forever's own client tables (the exports on [wago.tools](https://wago.tools)): zones and where
  positions go on their maps, factions and races (how a creature reacts to each side), locks (the skill
  a herb, a vein or a locked chest needs), items (which exist, vendor prices, quality and level for
  disenchanting), disenchanting's groups.
- [EG Link's output for WoW: Forever](https://github.com/JIVESCORP/eg-link-output-wowf) (Data from
  [Efficient Games](https://efficient.games), licensed under
  [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)): the positions players' games recorded with
  their zone map (which zone a spawn is on where zones' rectangles overlap) and how creatures reacted to
  players of each side. Changed: selected and converted to this format.

## What's in it, field by field

All in `LefthyToolsMoneyData` (`source`, `build`, then the tables below). Positions: x, y in thousandths
of the zone map (0 left/top, 1000 right/bottom), a cluster's radius r in thousandths of the map's width.
Chances in hundredths of a percent (`4785` = 47.85%). Each table's entries are strings, read only when
asked.

### Loot (in every table below)

`"item:chance[:count[:flag]],..."`, the likeliest first; count `min[-max]` (left out when it's 1); flag
`e`: only while a game event is on (Winter Squid, Raw Summer Bass), `A` / `H`: only for that side.

- A loot row on its own drops with its chance; in a group, one item of the group drops (rows without a
  chance share what's left of 100% evenly).
- A reference to another table rolls that table with the row's chance, as many times as its maxcount
  says (so the count can be up to that many times the inner count).
- An item reached several ways: 1 − the product of (1 − each way's chance).
- Left out: quest-only rows (negative chance), rows with a condition other than a game event, a side or
  a skill (quests, auras, areas, scripts), items Forever's client doesn't know, chances under 0.05%.

### mobs, zones: creatures worth farming

`mobs[creature] = "name|rank|type|skin|reactA|reactH|respawn|flags|rep|spots"`

- Which: creatures with something to loot (classic-db's `creature_template`: LootId, MaxLootGold,
  SkinningLootId or PickpocketLootId) and a spawn outside events. Not critters, totems, invisible ones,
  ones that can't be selected or attacked (UnitFlags 0x2000000, 0x2), triggers and unused ones (by name),
  ones that spawn only in battlegrounds, ones friendly to both sides, and ones of a faction with
  reputation that neither side's new characters are hostile with (killing them costs standing: Booty
  Bay's bruisers).
- `rank`: `creature_template.Rank`: `n` normal, `e` elite, `R` rare elite, `b` boss, `r` rare.
- `type`: `creature_template.CreatureType` (1 Beast, 2 Dragonkin, 3 Demon, 4 Elemental, 5 Giant, 6 Undead,
  7 Humanoid, 9 Mechanical, 10 not specified).
- `skin`: `l` when it can be skinned (SkinningLootId). Classic has no creatures gathered with Herbalism
  or Mining (CreatureTypeFlags 0x100 / 0x200 aren't set anywhere in the dump; the tool stops if one is).
- `reactA`, `reactH`: what the creature does to a new character of that side, `h` hostile, `n` neutral,
  `f` friendly; worked out the way the client does it (CMaNGOS' Relations.cpp names each function's
  client counterpart), with Forever's client tables:
  - a faction with reputation (`Faction.ReputationIndex` ≥ 0) reacts by the character's standing; a new
    character's is `Faction.ReputationBase` of the first of its four rows whose race mask (and class
    mask, if any) has the race. Up to −3001 hostile (hated, hostile), −3000 to 2999 neutral
    (unfriendly, neutral), 3000 or more friendly. Its faction's ID is then `rep`;
  - else the faction templates against the race's own (`ChrRaces.FactionID`): the creature's
    EnemyGroup or Enemies list has it: hostile; FriendGroup or Friend lists either way: friendly; else
    neutral;
  - every playable race (Forever's Skyborne too) and class of the side; when they differ (the High
    Order Skyborne start neutral with Ironforge), the friendliest;
  - where players' games on Forever saw a creature of a faction without reputation react one way to a
    side (EG Link's reactions, 1–2 hostile, 3–4 neutral, 5–8 friendly), that, with the flag `A` / `H`
    (Mesa Buzzards are hostile to the Horde on Forever; classic-db has them neutral). Not for factions
    with reputation: there it depends on who looked.
- `respawn`: seconds, the median over its spawns of (spawntimesecsmin + spawntimesecsmax) / 2 (classic's;
  Forever may differ).
- `flags`: `i` every spawn in a dungeon or raid, `c` a civilian (`creature_template.Civilian`), `A` / `H`
  above.
- `spots`: `"map,x,y,r,n[,up];..."`, at most 5 clusters, the most spawns first; `n` the spawn points, `up`
  about how many are up at once (left out when it's n). None: in dungeons, or outdoors where no zone map
  is (at sea).
- `zones[uiMap]`: the creatures with a spawn on that zone map.

### Spawns, places, clusters (mobs, nodes, vendors)

- A spawn is a row of `creature` / `gameobject`; a row with id 0 is one of the entries
  `creature_spawn_entry` / `gameobject_spawn_entry` (else `spawn_group_entry`) lists for it, each as
  likely (or by its Chance). Spawns only while a game event is on (`game_event_*`, event > 0) are left
  out.
- How many are up at once (`up`, an estimate): a pooled spawn counts min(1, max_limit / members) of its
  pool (`pool_template`, `pool_creature`, `pool_gameobject`), times its pool's own share of its mother
  pool (`pool_pool`); a limit of 0 means all; a spawn group's MaxCount the same way; a random entry its
  chance. Herbs and veins are pooled: Elwynn Forest has 81 Silverleaf spawn points, about 12 up at a
  time.
- Each spawn goes on the zone map the client has there, by the rules LefthyTools_QuestData uses
  (`places()` in tools/update-quest-data.js: the smallest zone rectangle holding it; where zones'
  rectangles overlap, the maps EG Link recorded this creature or object on, the positions players'
  games recorded nearby, the zones' explored-area images), then in clusters: spawns within 70
  thousandths of the map's width of a cluster's centre join it; the radius is the farthest one's
  distance plus 10 (at least 15, at most 150).

### objects, nodes: gathering

`objects[object] = "name|kind|skill|respawn|uses|loot"`, `nodes[uiMap] = "object:n:up=cluster;...|..."`
(cluster `"x,y,r,n[,up]"`, at most 12 per object and map; n the spawn points on the map, up about how
many are up at once).

- `herb`, `ore`: chests (gameobject type 3) whose lock (Forever's `Lock` row by data0) has a Herbalism
  (LockType 2) or Mining (3) entry; `skill` is that entry's Skill (Peacebloom 1, Mageroyal 50,
  Briarthorn 70; Copper 1, Tin 65, Silver 75, Iron 125).
- `chest`: a Treasure entry (LockType 6), not a quest object (flags 0x4): Battered Chests, footlockers,
  clams. `skill`: Lockpicking's, from its Pick Lock entry, when it's locked (flags 0x2), else 0.
- `fish`: fishing schools (type 25); the zone's fishing skill applies.
- `respawn`: the median of its spawns' times (pooled nodes come back elsewhere); `uses`: times one node
  can be looted, `min[-max]` (chests: data4..data5, the "min / max successful loot attempts"; schools
  data2..data3).
- `loot`: `gameobject_loot_template` by data1. Only objects with a spawn on a zone map.

### fishing, fishingAreas

`fishing[uiMap] = "base|loot"`, `fishingAreas[area] = "uiMap|name|base|loot"`.

- The rule (CMaNGOS' GameObject.cpp): the area's `skill_fishing_base_level` (the subzone's, else the
  zone's) is the base; a catch needs skill ≥ base and succeeds with (skill − base + 5)%, so it's sure
  from base + 95 (Elwynn Forest: base −70, sure from 25; Stranglethorn Vale: 130, sure from 225). The
  loot: the subzone's `fishing_loot_template`, else the zone's.
- A zone map's entry is its zone area's (the top-level area whose map it is); `fishingAreas` lists its
  subzones with their own base or loot.

### pickpocket

`pickpocket[creature] = loot`: `pickpocketing_loot_template` by PickpocketLootId, for the creatures in
`mobs`. (The coins a pocket holds aren't in classic-db's tables.)

### disenchant, disenchantLoot

`disenchant[item] = group` (`"group:skill"` when more than Enchanting 1 is needed), `disenchantLoot[group]
= loot`.

- Which items and the skill: Forever's client. An item it has (ItemSparse) can be disenchanted when its
  class (Item), quality and item level are in a row of `ItemDisenchantLoot` and it hasn't the "can't be
  disenchanted" flag (Flags_0 0x8000). The skill is that row's SkillRequired (0 in every row of this
  build: Enchanting learned is enough). Forever made some classic white items green (Handstitched
  Leather Pants): those can be disenchanted. Items the client's ItemSparse lacks: classic-db's
  DisenchantID and classic's class, quality and level.
- The results: classic-db's (`disenchant_loot_template`): the item's own group (`item_template
  .DisenchantID`, numbered otherwise than the client's rows: classic puts shields and off-hand items with
  the weapons), else the group most classic items (90% or more) of its client row and inventory type use.

### limited, vendors: limited vendor stock

`limited[item] = "price|count|basis|npc:stock:restock;..."`, `vendors[npc] = "name|reactA|reactH|map,x,y"`.

- `npc_vendor` rows with maxcount > 0, and `npc_vendor_template`'s for the vendors using it
  (VendorTemplateId): `stock` maxcount, `restock` incrtime (seconds).
- `price` in copper for `count` items: Forever's ItemSparse BuyPrice and VendorStackCount (`basis` f),
  else classic's BuyPrice and BuyCount (`c`). Before reputation discounts.
- The vendor: its name, its reactions (as for mobs: who can buy), its first spawn on a zone map.

## Making it again

From the LefthyTools repository:

```
node tools/update-money-data.js                          the latest Forever build, EG Link's newest
node tools/update-money-data.js --cache <dir>            keep the downloads in <dir>
node tools/update-money-data.js --dump <dump.sql[.gz]>   a classic dump you have
node tools/update-money-data.js --eglink <commit>        EG Link at that commit
node tools/update-money-data.js --stats                  only the numbers, nothing written
```

The tool checks samples known from the game before it writes (gathering skills, city guards' reactions,
fishing skills, disenchanting, a limited item) and stops if one doesn't hold. Run it when Forever gets
new builds.
